1. Affiliate and review claims — disclose the money

If a specialist desk (Compass) or a customer’s own site recommends a product for a fee, US advertising law treats that as an endorsement with a material connection.

  • Operator action: put the disclosure next to the claim, in the same language as the claim. “May earn a commission” in a footer is not enough when the claim is in the hero.
  • Cite: FTC Guides Concerning the Use of Endorsements and Testimonials in Advertising, 16 CFR Part 255, adopted 26 July 2023. Federal Register 2023-14795. FTC index: 16 CFR Part 255 notice.
  • Cite: FTC plain-language hub for endorsements, influencers, and reviews. ftc.gov/business-guidance/…/endorsements-influencers-reviews.
  • Bound: Compass never reuses a prior hop. Pulse never writes a fake review. This is not legal advice.

2. Public vendor standing — awards, not vibes

Docket’s paid pack is a public-record vendor standing file: USASpending award rollup plus a dated OFAC SDN named-entity screen. This sample shows the kind of citation, not a named-entity pack.

  • Operator action: name the legal entity exactly as it appears on the award, then search that string. Nicknames and DBAs are a second pass, never a substitute.
  • Cite: USAspending public award data. usaspending.gov.
  • Cite: SAM.gov entity information for federal registrants. sam.gov.
  • Cite: OFAC Specially Designated Nationals list. ofac.treasury.gov SDN lists.
  • Bound: Docket never files SAM, never submits a bid, and never gives legal, credit, or medical advice. Unpaid checkout is not a pack.
