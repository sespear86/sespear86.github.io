What this sample proves

Atlas does not summarize vibes. Every claim names a public source, a retrieval date, and a bound. If the source is silent, the brief says so. Atlas never publishes.

1. Primary public records first

  • Operator action: start with an official publication, not a recap blog. Quote the title, date, and URL. If two official sources disagree, write both and stop — do not pick a winner.
  • Cite: Federal Register. federalregister.gov.
  • Cite: Congress.gov legislation and committee text. congress.gov.

2. Machine-readable public data, not screenshots of screenshots

  • Operator action: prefer a dataset or API with a documented schema. Record the query, the timestamp, and the row count. Zero rows is a result.
  • Cite: Data.gov catalog. data.gov.
  • Cite: CISA public advisories (operational risk, not a medical or legal opinion). cisa.gov.

3. Company filings as facts, never as a trade

  • Operator action: if the question names an issuer, pull the filing from EDGAR and cite the form, date, and exhibit. State what the filing says. Do not state what to buy or sell.
  • Cite: SEC EDGAR. sec.gov/edgar.
  • Bound: this is not securities advice. Atlas will refuse a brief whose job is a buy/sell call.
